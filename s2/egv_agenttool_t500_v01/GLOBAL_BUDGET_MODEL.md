# 12-hour global budget model

Official Kaggle Evaluation says12 hours to submit patches, including sandbox setup and excluding validation. The current official data page says about120 test tasks split evenly public/private.129 is the public development count, not the hidden test count. Exact per-rerun task count, scoring order and concurrency are not exposed by the available public implementation. Therefore60/80/100/120/140/160 are sensitivity cases; no parallel speedup is credited in the conservative serial model.

GLOBAL_BUDGET_SCENARIOS.csv contains144 seeded Monte Carlo scenarios (1000 repetitions each): preparation5/20/60s,500s-cap fraction25/50/75/100%, early finish120/240s,600s global reserve, shuffled processing order. Phase2 is excluded from this12h model. Formal Kaggle platform runtime can impose an additional distinct window; model bootstrap/export overhead must not be assumed free. P50/P90/P95,cap/submission/unfinished-Patch proportions are assumptions, not observations. The tail/missing-Patch assumptions and lack of calibration are explicit in code/JSON. Different task order can privilege early tasks and induce repository/difficulty selection; no independence or uniform hidden difficulty is claimed. Concurrency2 is not assumed to halve time: model-service contention and deadlines make that inference unsafe.

At120 serial tasks, average Agent+setup time must stay at most360s before reserve; with600s reserve,355s. With20s setup the Agent mean must stay at most335s. If early completion takes120s, a500s-cap fraction above56.6% violates the mean envelope; if early completion takes240s, the threshold is36.5%. These are arithmetic scenario thresholds, not inferred Gemma probabilities. All-full-cap+20s setup permits only81 complete tasks with reserve, leaving39/120 unrun. At60 tasks even full cap is31200s plus600s and fits. Hidden split/rerun scheduling therefore materially changes risk.

Current measured T500 GPU sample size is0. Empirical duration quantiles, full-cap rate, explicit submission rate, delegation latency, retest reserve and actual exhaustion probability are null. The two earlier S1 cap-exhausted trajectories concern different architecture and may flag a concern, but cannot calibrate a T500 population model. CPU toy/mock durations likewise cannot calibrate Gemma latency. A fallback Patch and an explicit submission are distinct; missing Patch is also distinct from an incorrect Patch.

Submission risk criterion is registered before any T500 score: No-Go if verified scheduling and representative matched T500 observations support at least90% probability of at least20% unrun tasks. Conditional high-risk scenario frequencies alone are not an empirical probability. If actual scheduling/timings make the high-risk envelope applicable, stop rather than spend the daily submission. Otherwise one authorized exploratory official score can be considered after all technical/eligibility gates, with this unresolved global risk disclosed; this is not certification that all120 tasks fit or that500s is optimal. No claim of expected resolution gain is available.
